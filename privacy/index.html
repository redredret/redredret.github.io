1. Who we are

LDBG, the game at play.ldbg.net, its website ldbg.net and its Windows app, is made and run by DjYale, an individual developer in Connecticut, USA ("we", "us"). We decide what happens to the data described here, which makes us its "controller" under data-protection law. You can reach us at support@ldbg.net.

This policy covers the game and these websites. Purchases are sold by Tebex, which handles your payment under its own policy.

3. Your email address

  • Where it comes from. When you log in with Google, GitHub, Discord or an email login, your login tells our server your email address and whether it has been checked. The game never asks you to type it.
  • Kept up to date. If you change the address at your login provider, the game picks up the new one the next time you log in.
  • Who sees it. You see a shortened copy in the account menu, like j••••@gmail.com. The full address never goes to your browser or to other players. We look up one player's address only when we need to, and every look-up is logged.
  • What we use it for. Only to write to you about your own account: a refund, a dispute, a security problem, a moderation decision, or the game shutting down; and to answer you when you write to us. We send these by hand from support@ldbg.net. Your receipts come from Tebex, not from us.
  • What we don't do with it. No newsletters, no marketing mail, no selling or sharing it. If we ever want to send news, we'll ask first, with a choice that starts off, and update this policy.
  • How long. For as long as your account exists. Deleting your account deletes the address, and we keep nothing of it afterwards.

4. Purchases and payments

Purchases are sold by Tebex Limited, a company in England and Wales, as the seller. You pay on Tebex's own page, in a separate window. Tebex collects your payment details, billing address, country and whatever its checkout asks for, and decides what to do with them under its own privacy policy and terms. Tebex is a separate controller of that data, not someone working for us.

We never see or store your card or billing details. When our server opens a checkout, it tells Tebex only what you're buying, its price, and a number that lets our server match the payment to your checkout: not your name, email or account id. Tebex then tells our server that the payment went through, was refunded or was disputed, and our server keeps the record in the table above. Our server checks with Tebex once a day in case a message was missed.

5. Crash reports, bug reports and fair play

  • Crash and freeze reports are on unless you turn them off (Settings > Performance > Crash Reports; the choice follows your account). A report holds the game's version, your operating system and graphics card names, screen size, what you were doing in the game, frame times, memory use, error counts, the time since your last key press, your connection state, and the last 200 lines of the game's log. Before it leaves your device, anything that looks like a login token, a password or an email address is removed from it. Guests' reports are never sent.
  • Run-problem reports: if the server can't accept a run you finished, the game sends the version, your operating system name, screen size, connection state, and facts about the run, so we can find out why.
  • Bug reports from HELP > Reach out may include the game's recent log, only if you switch "Send My Recent Logs" on for that report. The words you write are kept as you wrote them, except that anything that looks like a password or login token is removed. Please don't put personal details in them.
  • Play statistics are about fair play in a run, and are never used for anything else. A person looks at what they show and decides. Nothing bans an account automatically.

We read these ourselves. They're never sent to an analytics or crash-reporting company.

6. Chat, friends and reports

  • Chat, where it's open to you: your lines, with your display name and level at the time. Everyone in that channel sees them. Our server checks every line against a word filter and rate limits.
  • Friends: your friends list and requests, and your online status, last-seen date and what you're playing, shown to your friends only. You can hide your activity, or appear offline, in Settings. We keep only the last-seen date, never a history.
  • Recent players: who you played with lately, and in what, so you can add them. Only you see this list.
  • Invites between friends, until they expire.
  • Blocks: who you've blocked. Only you see it; the player you block isn't told.
  • Reports you make about a name or a chat line, with a copy of that line and the ten before it, so the evidence isn't lost when chat is cleared. Only we see reports.

7. What other players can see

  • Your display name, your character's look and worn gear, your level, the badge you show, and your duel record, wherever the game shows you to others: the Market, the Arena, Farm rooms, parties, duels, boards of best runs, and "who took your gear" receipts.
  • Your Market listings, with your name, to anyone who opens the Market.
  • Your board during a Farm or party match, to anyone who knows the room's code. A board picture carries no name or account id.
  • Your chat lines to the channel; your online status and activity to your friends, unless you hide them; which room you're in, on a JOIN card, to a friend you invite.
  • Never your email, your account id, your purchases or anything in section 5.

8. What stays on your device

LDBG sets no cookies, and has no analytics or advertising scripts. It keeps a few things in your browser's storage, each because the game needs it or you chose it:

  • Your preferences: controls, handling, volume, font, performance, colour-blind mode, which skin you wear, and whether crash reports are sent. One volume number is also kept so the loading screen plays at your volume.
  • A small picture of your character's head, for the browser tab's icon.
  • Reports waiting to be sent (crash reports and run-problem reports), until they're sent or dropped.
  • The moves of one unfinished dungeon run, so a run cut off by closing the tab can still be settled next time. It's deleted once the run settles.
  • Sign-in: for the ten minutes a login takes, a few one-time security values in the tab's session storage, deleted as soon as you're logged in. Your login itself is kept only in the tab's memory, and is gone when you close the tab.

The Windows app also keeps its window size and position, its own settings, and any crash report waiting to be sent, in your Windows user folder. It checks GitHub for updates.

The sign-in service run by Clockwork Labs sets its own cookies to keep you logged in there. Those are theirs, under their policy.

Because nothing here tracks you, the game doesn't need a cookie banner. If that ever changes, we'll ask first.

9. What we don't collect

  • Your real name, address, phone number, birthday or age.
  • Your card or bank details (Tebex has those).
  • Your IP address. Our game server isn't told it. The companies that host the websites and the server see it as part of running them (see section 11).
  • Your contacts, location, camera or microphone.
  • Anything from other websites or apps you use.

Your login provider also tells the game your login's name. That stays in your browser's memory for the account menu and isn't stored on our server.

10. Our reasons, in law

If you're in the EU, the UK or somewhere with similar law, these are our legal reasons for using your data:

  • To give you the game you signed up for (a contract): your account, progress, items, multiplayer, purchases, and mail about your account.
  • Our legitimate interests, which we've weighed against yours: checking runs and spotting bots, keeping chat and names safe, crash reports (which you can turn off), the few records kept after deletion (so a suspension can't be dodged and a purchase dispute can still be matched), and security.
  • The law: records we must keep about money, and answering lawful requests.
  • Your choice: sending the log with a bug report, and showing your activity to friends.

13. Deleting your account

Your account, email, progress, items, purchases (which aren't refunded), friends, chat lines, messages, reports you made, replays, settings and your agreement to the Terms are deleted at once. Other players see "Deleted player" where your name was in their own history.

14. Your rights, and how to use them

Depending on where you live, you can ask us to:

  • see the data we keep about you, and get a copy;
  • correct it (you can rename yourself in the game; your email follows your login);
  • delete it (the DELETE ACCOUNT button does this straight away);
  • stop or limit some uses, such as crash reports (Settings turns them off);
  • take it with you in a readable file.

Write to support@ldbg.net from the address your login uses, and tell us your display name, so we can check the account is yours. We answer within a month. We don't charge, and we'll never treat you worse for asking.

If you're in the EU or the UK, you can also complain to your data-protection authority (in the UK, the ICO). If you're in California, you have these rights under California law too, and we don't sell or share personal information as that law defines it.

15. Children

LDBG is for players 13 and over. We don't knowingly keep data about children under 13. If we learn that an account belongs to one, we delete it. A parent who thinks their child under 13 has an account can write to support@ldbg.net, and we'll delete it. Players under 18 need a parent's or guardian's permission to buy anything.

16. Where your data is

The game's server, its sign-in, the websites and our support inbox are in the United States, so your data is handled there, wherever you play from. Tebex handles payments from the UK. Where the law asks for it, we rely on the safeguards these companies provide for moving data between countries.

17. Keeping it safe

  • The game keeps your login only in memory, never in a file, and never sends it anywhere but the sign-in and the game's server.
  • Player data lives in private tables that only our server code can read. Other players get only what section 7 lists.
  • The accounts that control the game use two-step login, and the online backups are encrypted.

No system is perfectly safe. If a leak puts you at risk, we'll tell you, and the authorities where the law requires, as quickly as we can.

18. Changes to this policy

When the game changes what it keeps or why, we update this policy and the date at the top. If a change matters, we'll say so in the game before it takes effect, and ask again where the law needs your consent.

19. Contact

Email support@ldbg.net for anything about your data. There's also our Discord, but please don't post personal details there.
